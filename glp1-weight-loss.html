What is the difference between semaglutide and tirzepatide?

Semaglutide activates one metabolic pathway — the GLP-1 receptor. Tirzepatide activates two: GLP-1 and GIP. The dual mechanism is why tirzepatide showed higher average weight loss in clinical trials (up to 22% vs. approximately 15% for semaglutide). Both are once-weekly injections. Your Foundry provider will recommend the right compound based on your health profile, goals, and how your body responds over time.

Is compounded semaglutide the same as Ozempic or Wegovy?

Compounded semaglutide contains the same active molecule as Ozempic and Wegovy, prepared by a licensed 503B outsourcing facility rather than a brand-name pharmaceutical manufacturer. It is not an FDA-approved finished drug product. Foundry RX compounds exclusively through 503B-certified pharmacies — the highest federal standard — with batch-testing above 99.9% purity on every lot.

How much weight can I realistically expect to lose?

Clinical trials of brand-name semaglutide showed average weight loss of approximately 15% of body weight over 68 weeks when combined with dietary and lifestyle modifications. Tirzepatide trials showed up to 22% average weight reduction. Results are highly individual — starting weight, adherence, and titration all matter. Your Foundry provider will set realistic expectations for your specific profile at intake.

What is a 503B pharmacy and why does it matter?

A 503B outsourcing facility is a federally registered compounding pharmacy operating under FDA oversight and Current Good Manufacturing Practice (CGMP) standards — the same standard applied to conventional pharmaceutical manufacturers. This is significantly more stringent than a traditional 503A compounding pharmacy. Foundry RX partners exclusively with 503B-certified facilities and batch-tests every lot above 99.9% purity before it ships.

Important safety information

GLP-1 receptor agonists including compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide may have serious side effects, including possible thyroid tumors. Do not use if you or your family have a history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) or MEN 2. Compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide are not FDA-approved drugs. All medications prescribed at independent clinical judgment of licensed physicians. Common side effects include nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, constipation, and abdominal discomfort.
